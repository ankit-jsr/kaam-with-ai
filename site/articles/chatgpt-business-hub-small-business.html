ChatGPT Has a New Business Hub. What Small Business Owners Can Actually Do With It.

KaamWithAI Editorial · 14 November 2026 · Approximately 8 minute read

A customer is comparing three businesses. They ask ChatGPT which one looks right.

Until recently, a business owner had very little direct control over the information ChatGPT might use in that answer. The system could draw on public websites and other sources, and the owner could correct their own website but not manage a dedicated business profile inside ChatGPT.

OpenAI is now adding that missing layer.

Its Business Hub lets eligible businesses claim a profile, update details, share product information and see some aggregate signals about how their business appears in ChatGPT. A separate feature, Business Agents, lets a narrower group of businesses offer a dedicated AI conversation about their products and policies.

That sounds like a big shift. It is—but not quite in the way the launch headlines might suggest.

Claiming a profile does not buy a better position in ChatGPT's recommendations. The current Help Center is explicit that claiming a profile or disputing ownership does not change its visibility in ChatGPT. It gives an eligible owner a way to manage business information; it does not control the system's independent recommendations. An agent cannot currently take a payment, make a booking or look up an individual order. And the agent feature is currently limited to eligible ecommerce and online-service businesses in the United States.

For a small-business owner, the useful question is not “How do I get ChatGPT to recommend me?” It is: “What information can I keep accurate, what can I learn from the new insights, and what should I not expect this system to do?”

First, understand the three separate pieces

Business Hub is the place where an eligible owner or authorized representative manages a business profile. That profile can include the website, description, category, logo, delivery policy and return policy.

Retailers can also share product details, prices and availability through a product feed. Shopify is one route, but OpenAI says a Shopify store is not required; eligible businesses can also upload a CSV feed that follows its specification.

Business Agents are different. They are optional, separate customer-facing experiences that answer questions using information and instructions supplied by the business. Publishing an agent is not automatic when a profile is claimed.

It helps to keep those three pieces distinct:

  • Profile: the business information an owner can manage.
  • Product feed: structured product details, including prices and availability.
  • Business Agent: a separate AI conversation about the business's products and policies.

You may be eligible for one and not another.

Who can actually use it?

This is where the launch needs a little context.

OpenAI says eligible businesses in locations where ChatGPT is offered can claim a profile, subject to business type, location, verification and integrity checks. You must own the business or be authorized to represent it. The available verification methods can include a business phone, business email, website-domain DNS record or Shopify.

There is an additional identity-verification step through Persona before an owner can publish profile changes, connect a product feed, view insights or create an eligible agent.

Business Agents have a narrower rollout: they are currently available to eligible ecommerce and online-service businesses in the United States, with a website and an industry that meets OpenAI's policy. OpenAI's Help Center also says local stores and restaurants can currently claim only an existing US listing.

So if you run a shop, restaurant or service business in India, don't assume you can publish a Business Agent today. Check the current eligibility flow for your business before spending time preparing one. You may be able to claim a profile in some circumstances, but the agent rollout is not worldwide.

What can an owner control?

The most useful part of Business Hub is also the least glamorous: keeping the facts straight.

An owner can update core business information such as the website, description, category, logo and policies. Retailers can provide a product feed so customers can see product details, prices and availability in ChatGPT.

That matters because a business can have outdated or inconsistent information scattered across its website, catalogues and public listings. If a customer asks about delivery, returns or whether a product is available, the answer is only as useful as the information the system can access.

A practical first pass is to check:

  • Is the business name and website correct?
  • Does the description explain what you actually sell and who you serve?
  • Are the service categories and locations accurate?
  • Are delivery, cancellation, warranty and return policies current?
  • If you sell products, do prices and availability match the live store?

Don't treat the profile as a marketing campaign. Treat it as a source of business facts that should be accurate.

OpenAI reviews changes before publishing them, and says updates can take time to appear. ChatGPT may also use other sources when answering questions about a business.

What do the new insights tell you?

After the claim and identity verification are approved, Business Hub can show aggregate activity over the previous 28 days. The Help Center describes metrics including mentions, website clicks and discovery topics.

A mention is not simply someone typing your brand name. For an online business, OpenAI says a mention is counted when ChatGPT references the business's website. For a local business, it is counted when ChatGPT shows a result for that location, even if the user does not open the listing or visit the website.

Discovery topics group themes in answers where the business appeared. They do not show individual customer prompts or directly measure purchase intent. Businesses do not get access to individual prompts or conversations with their Business Agents.

There is another detail worth knowing: a blank metric does not necessarily mean zero activity. OpenAI says it can mean activity has not reached the reporting threshold, data is still being collected or reporting is unavailable.

These insights are best treated as directional signals. They may help an owner understand whether ChatGPT is sending people to the website and what broad themes are associated with the business. They are not a complete analytics product or a record of every customer question.

What can a Business Agent do—and what can't it do?

An eligible business can create a separate AI agent that answers questions using the business information and instructions supplied by the owner. Think of it as a product-and-policy explainer: a customer can ask about an offering, compare options or understand a return policy, and the agent can point them to the business website for the next step.

But it is not a complete customer-service or commerce system.

OpenAI's current Help Center says the agent cannot complete purchases, make bookings or check an individual customer's order. Those actions happen on the business website or through a separate supported product.

That distinction should shape how an owner thinks about the feature.

A sensible first job might be answering repeat questions about product specifications, delivery areas, opening information or return rules. A poor first job would be promising that the agent can resolve a delayed order when it has no access to the order system.

OpenAI's policy also requires agents to represent the business honestly, keep information accurate and make clear that the agent is AI. It says agents must not invent prices, availability, credentials or results, pressure users, collect unnecessary high-risk personal information or make high-stakes decisions without human review.

The owner remains responsible for the information and instructions provided.

Does claiming a profile make ChatGPT recommend you more?

No guarantee—and this is probably the most important point in the whole launch.

OpenAI says claiming a business lets the owner manage the information provided about the business. It does not guarantee increased visibility or give the owner direct control over ChatGPT's independent recommendations. Advertising does not influence those independent answers either.

In other words, Business Hub is not a paid ranking switch. It is not a replacement for your website, customer reviews, reputation or the work of making your business easy to understand.

It gives eligible businesses a new place to keep information current and, in some cases, a new way to answer customer questions. That is useful. It is not a promise of traffic or sales.

A practical checklist before you do anything

If Business Hub is available for your business, use this order:

  1. Check eligibility. Confirm that your business type and location can claim a profile. Check Business Agent eligibility separately.
  2. Prepare proof of authorization. You may need access to a business phone or email, website DNS settings or a Shopify account. Persona identity verification is required for several management features.
  3. Audit your public facts. Make sure your website, categories, service areas, product information and policies are accurate before updating another profile.
  4. Keep product data in sync. If you connect a feed, make sure prices and availability match your actual storefront.
  5. Test an agent with awkward questions. If eligible, test stock, delivery, returns, discounts, exceptions and edge cases. Fix the underlying information when an answer is wrong.
  6. Give customers a human route. Complaints, unusual requests and order-specific problems should reach a person or a system that can actually resolve them.
  7. Read insights carefully. Treat mentions, clicks and discovery topics as directional signals, not proof of purchase intent or a complete view of demand.

The sensible takeaway

ChatGPT is creating more ways for businesses to provide current information directly and, for some, to offer a separate AI-powered customer conversation.

That is worth paying attention to. But the immediate job for most small businesses is not to chase an AI ranking trick. It is to make sure the information customers—and the systems helping them make decisions—can find is accurate, useful and consistent.

If you can claim a profile, do the basics carefully. If you cannot, don't panic or buy a service promising guaranteed ChatGPT visibility. Keep your website and product information clear, and revisit eligibility as the rollout changes.

The new Business Hub is a useful control surface. It is not a shortcut around the fundamentals of running a good business.
