# Echo Janitorial: AI for the work that happens between jobs

**Research date:** 18 November 2026  
**Status:** Research brief; no article draft or publication approval yet  
**Potential format:** Reported field guide / operator story  
**Audience:** Owners of cleaning, trades and other local service businesses whose work takes them away from the phone and inbox

## Editorial premise

A cleaning-company owner cannot answer every call while working at a client site. Rick Chorney, co-founder of Echo Janitorial Services in Abbotsford, British Columbia, told Fortune that he connected new customer enquiries to his job-management system, added an AI receptionist and set up automatic acknowledgements. The reported workflow is useful because it begins with a familiar business problem: what happens to an enquiry when the owner is busy doing the paid work?

The story should explain the handoff from call or form to job system and human follow-up. It should not claim that AI created a million-dollar business.

## Main source

[Fortune — Meet a 29-year-old blue-collar founder who used AI to triple his revenue in 3 years](https://fortune.com/2026/03/28/ai-small-business-entrepreneur-1-million-blue-collar/)  
By Nick Lichtenberg, 28 March 2026.

Fortune reports an interview with Rick Chorney, co-founder and president of Echo Janitorial Services. The company launched in 2023 and is based in Abbotsford, British Columbia. Fortune says it reviewed the company's business records to verify the reported revenue figures.

### What the article reports

- Chorney automated the customer intake form so new enquiries flowed into the company's job-management platform.
- He installed an AI receptionist and automatic acknowledgement messages for new clients. The initial setup reportedly took half a day.
- The receptionist is Jobber's product. Chorney says it handles up to 15 calls per hour and escalates matters that need attention.
- Chorney says Fixer AI sorts his inbox into action required, drafted reply, likely spam and confirmed spam, then texts a daily briefing.
- He uses Claude for BC labour-law research when HR situations become complicated, client-facing case studies and documenting operating procedures.
- He uses ChatGPT to polish emails and format documents, Perplexity for research, Grok for content creation and was piloting Synthesia for employee training videos.
- He told Fortune he now works about eight hours a day and spends around 20 minutes on email. These are his own reported figures.

The article contains more of Chorney's personal history than a KaamWithAI article needs. Keep the focus on the business workflow and the owner's operational choices.

## Revenue claims: use with care

Fortune reports:
- 2024 revenue: $242,000.
- 2025 revenue: just under $1 million.
- 2026 revenue: projected to exceed $1.3 million in the March 2026 article.

Fortune explicitly says it reviewed company records for the revenue figures. This is stronger than an unverified social-media claim, but it is not a public audit or a full set of financial statements.

**Do not claim AI caused the growth.** Chorney says he added AI agents and they helped him speed up quoting and hire more workers. The article also describes his sales background, earlier SEO work, expansion of the cleaning operation and hiring. The reported revenue trend does not isolate AI as the cause.

Do not use the Fortune headline's “tripled in 3 years” as a precise calculation. The reported 2024-to-2025 comparison is from $242,000 to just under $1 million, while the company was founded in 2023. State the source's actual dates and figures if they are needed.

## Vendor and independence caveat

Jobber powers the receptionist in the Fortune story, and Jobber co-founder/CTO Forrest Zeisler is quoted in the article. Fortune also cites Jobber's broader customer-growth claim. This does not invalidate the reported business workflow or Fortune's revenue-record review, but it is relevant commercial context.

If the story is published:
- Identify Jobber as the vendor behind the receptionist.
- Attribute Jobber's claims to Jobber.
- Do not treat this as an independent product test.
- Separate the owner's account of his business from vendor-wide performance claims.
- Avoid repeating the “90% faster growth” claim as an independently established fact.

## What the story can teach

The useful sequence for a local service operator is:

1. Capture the enquiry through a form or call.
2. Put the information into the system used to manage jobs.
3. Send a prompt acknowledgement so the customer knows the request arrived.
4. Let the receptionist answer routine questions within defined limits.
5. Escalate unusual requests, pricing decisions and commitments to a person.
6. Review the conversation and make sure the lead has a clear next step.

This is an editorial framework, not a tested implementation guide. Jobber's documentation confirms the current product can answer enquiries and book jobs within configured boundaries; it does not verify Chorney's exact configuration or performance.

## Evidence gaps

We do not have:
- A missed-call baseline or call-to-booking conversion rate.
- A measured count of enquiries captured by the receptionist.
- An independently measured reduction in admin time.
- A test of the receptionist's accuracy or customer experience.
- Independent verification of the 15-calls-per-hour claim.
- Details of the exact intake-form fields or the full mapping into Jobber.
- A breakdown of revenue, costs or profit attributable to each AI workflow.

Keep those limits visible. Do not infer a return on investment from revenue growth alone.
