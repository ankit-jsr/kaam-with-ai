# 3R Plumbing & Mechanical — 36 workflows built with Claude and Zapier

**Research date:** 20 November 2026  
**Status:** Research candidate for a future LinkedIn post or website field report  
**Primary source:** Zapier customer story, published 14 October 2026 — https://zapier.com/blog/3r-plumbing-next-gen-zaps/  
**Source type:** Vendor-authored customer story. Claims are reported by Zapier; no independent interview or system inspection.

## What the story reports

Ross Hunt started 3R Plumbing & Mechanical, a residential plumbing and HVAC company in Norwell, Massachusetts, in 2022. Zapier says the company grew from a garage operation to 15 employees, serving roughly 21 South Shore towns.

Hunt began using classic Zaps in 2022. In August 2026 he joined Zapier's Next-Gen Zaps early-access program. He describes telling Claude what workflow he wants; Claude, connected through Zapier MCP, drafts and publishes the automation. Hunt says he reviews the logic, tests it and checks where the data lands.

As of the article:

- 36 Next-Gen Zaps are live, running around 1,400 times per week.
- Zapier reports 30 connected apps.
- ServiceTitan is the system of record. Zapier sits between it and other apps.
- Role-specific portals surface work for customer service, sales, warehouse/purchasing, marketing and the owner.
- The customer-service callback queue refreshes every 15 minutes; the owner dashboard refreshes hourly.
- A weekday 6:30 a.m. email briefs the owner and operations manager on the day's board, callbacks and capacity.
- Alerts flag leads or bookings that remain pending, including a 20-minute check and a separate 24-hour check.
- Landing-page leads can create a ServiceTitan booking, notify the owner and send the customer a confirmation.

Zapier also reports the business began at around $300,000 annual revenue and is on track for nearly $3.8 million in 2026. **Do not attribute that growth to AI or automation.** The story covers the business's growth since 2022 and does not isolate AI as its cause.

## The useful editorial angle

The most useful lesson is not “a plumber built 36 AI agents.” The story is about making workflows legible and connected:

1. Keep one system of record (ServiceTitan).
2. Let an automation layer move and surface information.
3. Give each role the view and queue it actually needs.
4. Build alerts around the points where a lead or job can go stale.
5. Review, test and verify each workflow before relying on it.

Possible headline: **A plumbing owner built 36 AI-assisted workflows. His rule was to keep one system in charge of the data.**

## Evidence and editorial limits

- Vendor customer story, so Zapier has an incentive to highlight success.
- Reported figures (36 workflows, 1,400 runs/week, 30 connected apps, revenue) are not independently verified.
- No quantified time savings are given.
- The revenue figures describe overall company growth; no AI causality is established.
- The practical setup is more accurately described as AI-assisted automation using Claude, Zapier and ServiceTitan, not 36 autonomous agents.
- Avoid suggesting the setup is inexpensive, plug-and-play or suitable for every small business without assessing existing software, permissions, reliability and maintenance.
