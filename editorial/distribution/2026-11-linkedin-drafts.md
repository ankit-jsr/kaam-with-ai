## Post 1 — The distinction most coverage will miss

OpenAI's new Business Hub is interesting. But the important detail is that it is not one feature.

There are three separate things:

- A business profile that eligible owners can claim and keep accurate.
- Product information and feeds that can help keep details such as prices and availability current.
- Business Agents, a separate feature currently limited to eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses.

That distinction matters.

Claiming a profile does not mean ChatGPT will recommend your business more often. And publishing an agent does not mean it can take payments, make bookings or check a customer's order.

For a small business, the practical first step is less glamorous: make sure the information customers find about you is accurate, consistent and up to date.

AI discovery is changing. The basics of being clear about what you sell are not.

We're working through what business owners can actually do with the new tools — and what is still outside their control.

## Post 2 — A useful question for business owners

If a customer asked ChatGPT to compare your business with three competitors, would the answer have the right facts?

Not your marketing slogan. The useful facts:

- What exactly do you sell?
- Who do you serve, and where?
- What does delivery or service coverage look like?
- What are your return, cancellation or warranty policies?
- Are the prices and availability shown online current?

OpenAI's new Business Hub gives some eligible businesses a way to manage information shown in ChatGPT. It does not guarantee better visibility or let an owner control ChatGPT's independent recommendations.

Still, this is a good moment to audit the information your business publishes across its website and other public listings.

A practical AI-readiness exercise: ask someone unfamiliar with your business to find those answers using only your website. If they struggle, an AI system may struggle too.

## Post 3 — The boundary of a business agent

An AI business agent sounds like it should be able to run customer service end to end.

The current reality is narrower.

OpenAI's Business Agents can answer questions about a business's products and policies using information supplied by the business. They can direct customers to the business website.

They cannot currently complete purchases, make bookings or check an individual customer's order.

That boundary is useful to understand before getting excited about automation.

A good first use is answering repeat questions accurately: product details, delivery policies, returns, opening information and service coverage.

A poor use is promising customers that an agent can resolve an order issue when it cannot access the order system.

Automate the explanation. Keep a clear route to a human for exceptions.

That's a more useful starting point than trying to automate everything.
